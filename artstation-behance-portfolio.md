# SL3D — ArtStation & Behance portfolio content pack

Paste-ready text and an upload map for building Sam Lepley's **ArtStation** and
**Behance** artist portfolios. Same job as `linkedin-company-page.md`, different
platform: LinkedIn was a *company page* (client-facing); these two are *artist
portfolios* (recruiter- and client-facing). Set the accounts up, then work top to
bottom through this file.

Voice = the casual, first-person tone Sam approved for SL3D (see the LinkedIn pack).
Facts pulled from `src/data/projects.ts` — no invented numbers.

**Credential rule (non-negotiable, cf. `sl3d-copy-not-an-architect`):** never the
word "architect" / "architecture" as a personal title or discipline. "Architectural
visualisation" and "architectural rendering" are fine (they name the *subject* —
buildings — not a title). Discipline framing = *construction, interior design and
product visualisation*.

**Employment rule (cf. `sam-employment-status`):** Nubuiten, Outdoor Life Group and
BCD Travel are **past clients / in-house engagements**, never current employers.
Everything reads as completed past work.

## 0. Which platform matters more

For archviz/3D hiring, **ArtStation is the primary** — it's where studios and
recruiters actually look, and its tags/categories drive discovery. **Behance** is the
secondary net (Adobe ecosystem, more design/interiors clients, good SEO). The project
content below is written once and reused on both; only the *field names* differ, and
those are mapped per project.

**Title:** `Old Mutual Box — Loftus Versfeld`

**Description:**
> This one was personal — Loftus Versfeld was my old stomping ground from schoolboy
> rugby in Pretoria, so walking back in for work was a strange feeling.

### P5 — Scene Revival  *(AI regrade — before/after)*

**Title:** `Scene Revival — Regrading an Archive Render With AI`

**Description:**
> I rendered this garden room a couple of years back. The model still holds up fine — it
> was really the lighting that had dated.
>
> So rather than rebuild the whole scene, I ran the original through an AI regrade, kept
> it on a tight art-direction leash, and checked it back against the source model.
> Proportions, materials, all the product detail — left alone. The only thing that
> actually changed is the light. Same geometry, proper golden hour.
>
> A genuinely handy way to bring an older archive back up to scratch.

**Software:** AI regrade pipeline, original V-Ray render, Photoshop, Lightroom
**ArtStation category:** Product Design / Visualization
**Behance fields:** 3D Art, Photography *(the before/after reads as a photographic regrade)*
**Tags:** `ai-assisted, ai rendering, regrade, before after, golden hour, product visualization, garden room, photoreal, relighting, look development, cgi, 3d rendering`
**Images:** two-image before/after — `ai-revival/before.jpg` (LEFT, flat daylight
original), `ai-revival/after.jpg` (RIGHT/cover, AI golden-hour regrade). Both square.

## 4. Covers, tags, and the discovery game

- **ArtStation covers** should be the single strongest frame of each project — thumbnails
  are what get clicked in the browse/search feeds. Every P#'s cover above is chosen for that.
- **Tag generously on ArtStation** (it's how art directors find you); Behance caps tags, so
  lead with the first ~10.
- Once live, **submit the two best (5 Apple Road, Old Mutual Box) to the relevant ArtStation
  channels** (Archviz) — channel features are the main discovery driver there.
- Behance: attach each project to the right **Creative Fields** and it flows into those
  category feeds.
